I build and study augmented, extended, and mixed reality simulations with embodied virtual humans, and I use them to train the people who care for older adults.

My work sits at the meeting point of human–computer interaction, healthcare simulation, and applied AI. I prototype spatially aware virtual patients on the Microsoft HoloLens 2 under a National Science Foundation award, then evaluate them with mixed methods — usability, technology acceptance, and social presence studies alongside interviews with professional caregivers.

About

Caregiving for older adults is a soft-skills profession. Technical competence matters, but so does how a caregiver speaks, listens, waits, and reads a room — and those skills are difficult to rehearse safely. My research asks whether a mixed reality patient can stand in for that rehearsal.

The virtual humans I build are designed to be aware: they track where the caregiver is, what the caregiver is doing, and respond with context-appropriate non-verbal behavior without a human operator hidden behind the curtain. Removing that operator is the central technical problem of my dissertation, and the reason the work is measurable — an automated system can be benchmarked against a human-controlled baseline on response accuracy and latency, and then tested with real caregivers.

Before NJIT I worked on machine learning for hyperspectral satellite imagery at RUET in Bangladesh, developing dimensionality-reduction pipelines for image classification. That background still shapes how I approach evaluation: build the system, then measure it honestly.

I have authored nine peer-reviewed conference publications, seven as first author, at venues including IEEE ISMAR, IEEE VR, and ACM IVA. I have taught undergraduates at NJIT for four years, including a semester as sole instructor of record.

Research

Three threads run through my work: making virtual humans aware of their surroundings, putting them in front of real caregivers, and measuring what actually changes.

  • Dissertation · In progress

    Aware Intelligent Virtual Humans in Mixed Reality

    Automating spatial awareness in embodied virtual humans so that context-appropriate non-verbal behavior is generated without a human operator in the loop, then benchmarking that automation against a human-controlled baseline on response accuracy and latency.

  • NSF FW-HTF · 2022–present

    Embodied Virtual Human Simulation for Caregiver Training

    Student team lead on a collaborative NSF project spanning NJIT, the University of Central Florida, and the University of Delaware. Prototyped an interactive AR/XR/MR simulation featuring a spatially aware geriatric virtual patient for communication and soft-skills training.

  • Methods

    Mixed-Methods Evaluation of Immersive Systems

    Quantitative studies of system usability, technology acceptance, social presence, empathy, attitudes, and self-efficacy, paired with interviews of professional caregivers analyzed through thematic analysis to feed the next design cycle.

  • Earlier work

    Dimensionality Reduction for Hyperspectral Imaging

    Hybrid feature selection and extraction pipelines (PCA, LDA, mRMR, nMI) paired with SVM, KNN, and ANN classifiers for multi-band satellite imagery. Three peer-reviewed IEEE and ACM publications.
