Dimensionality Reduction for Hyperspectral Image Classification

Rajshahi University of Engineering & Technology, Bangladesh · with Dr. Md. Al Mehedi Hasan

Three peer-reviewed IEEE and ACM conference papers on the Indian Pines scene.

  • Code on GitHub
  • All projects
  • Publications

The Indian Pines scene carries 220 spectral bands and nowhere near enough labelled pixels to use them, so reducing the bands should help, and naïve reduction makes things worse. Three papers work the problem from three angles; the last one beats the full-band baseline by 6.4 points.

In plain terms

Undergraduate research: three papers, one stubborn problem.

Satellites photograph farmland in 220 separate colour bands rather than the usual three. That extra detail ought to make telling one crop from another easy — except that with only a few thousand labelled pixels available, 220 measurements per pixel is far too many, and accuracy collapses. Statisticians call this the curse of dimensionality.

Each of the three papers attacks it from a different angle. The third one finally beats the do-nothing baseline.

The problem

The Hughes phenomenon

Also called the curse of dimensionality.

Hundreds of contiguous spectral bands, most of them highly correlated with their neighbours, and far fewer labelled training pixels than bands. The classifier generalises badly and accuracy falls as dimensionality rises, so the bands have to come down, without taking the class structure with them.

Captured by the AVIRIS sensor over north-western Indiana; roughly two thirds agriculture.

Three attempts on the same problem

Each paper takes the previous one’s weakness as its starting point.

  1. Feb 2019 · ECCE

    Does supervision help?

    PCA (unsupervised, 21 components) against LDA (supervised, capped at eight features) against both concatenated. LDA generalised best to the held-out test set, at 86.43% from a 96.4% cut in dimensionality, and concatenating the two was the worst of the three.

  2. Jul 2019 · IC4ME2

    Does chaining help?

    Feature extraction into feature selection: PCA first, then mRMR on what PCA produced. The hybrid took the best overall accuracy at 87.72%, from 21 features against mRMR’s 160.

  3. Jan 2020 · ICCA

    What if the scene is not one pool?

    Split the pixels by class, run PCA inside each class, take sixteen components from each and stack them back together. Overall accuracy 95.44%, which is 6.4 points above using all 220 bands, and nearly ten above plain PCA.

What the three papers add up to

  • The counter-intuitive result

    Reduction can cost you accuracy

    Plain PCA on 21 components scored more than three points below simply keeping all 220 bands. Variance is not the same thing as class separability.

  • What fixed it

    Preserve class structure through the reduction

    Running PCA per class keeps the within-class variance that a scene-wide projection averages away, and turns the same idea into a 6.4-point gain.

  • Supervision earns its keep

    Eight features, best generalisation

    LDA, which uses the labels, gave the strongest independent-test accuracy from a third as many features as PCA needed, though it loses to PCA on overall accuracy across the full data.

  • More data, cleaner classes

    The largest classes separate best

    The two classes with the highest area under the ROC curve were also the two with the most training pixels, at 714 and 1,227.
