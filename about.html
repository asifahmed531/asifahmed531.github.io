About me

Ph.D. candidate in Information Systems at NJIT, working on mixed reality simulation for caregiver training.

Caregiving for older adults is a soft-skills profession. Technical competence matters, but so does how a caregiver speaks, listens, waits, and reads a room — and those skills are difficult to rehearse safely. My research asks whether a mixed reality patient can stand in for that rehearsal.

The virtual humans I build are designed to be aware: they track where the caregiver is, what the caregiver is doing, and respond with context-appropriate non-verbal behavior without a human operator hidden behind the curtain. Removing that operator is the central technical problem of my dissertation, and the reason the work is measurable — an automated system can be benchmarked against a human-controlled baseline on response accuracy and latency, and then tested with real caregivers.

Before NJIT I worked on machine learning for hyperspectral satellite imagery at RUET in Bangladesh, developing dimensionality-reduction pipelines for image classification. That background still shapes how I approach evaluation: build the system, then measure it honestly.

I have authored ten peer-reviewed conference publications, seven as first author, at venues including IEEE ISMAR, IEEE VR, and ACM IVA. I have taught undergraduates at NJIT for four years, including a semester as sole instructor of record.
